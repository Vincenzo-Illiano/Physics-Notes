In this topic, we will introduce a series of corrections to the hydrogen atom orbitals and energy levels, in order to determine what is referred as **hydrogen fine structure**. The reason why this topic is studied is that the hydrogen atom is one of the simplest real scenarios where quantum mechanics can be applied and experimentally tested. 

We will in particular look at the following corrections:
- [[Relativistic correction - Hydrogen atom |Relativistic correction]]: to account for a relativistic term in the kinetic energy.
- [[Spin-Orbit interaction - Hydrogen atom |Spin-orbit interaction]]: To account for the interaction between the magnetic dipole generated by the electron due to its spin and the proton.
- [[Darwin term - Hydrogen atom|Darwin term]]: To account for *zitterbewegung*, an effect which can be derived from the Dirac equation.

## All the effects combined

We can combine al the effect described into a single corrected formula for the energy. For $\ell>0$, the Darwin shift is zero. In units of $E_n^{(0)}\alpha^2/n^2$, the terms proportional to $n$ combine as
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{n}{\ell+\tfrac12}
-\frac{n}{2(\ell+\tfrac12)(\ell+1)}
&=\frac{n}{\ell+1}, && j=\ell+\tfrac12,\\
\frac{n}{\ell+\tfrac12}
+\frac{n}{2\ell(\ell+\tfrac12)}
&=\frac{n}{\ell}, && j=\ell-\tfrac12.
\end{aligned}
$$
And both expressions simply evaulate to $n/(j+\tfrac12)$.

For $\ell=0$, spin–orbit gives zero and $j=\tfrac12$. The relativistic term contributes $2n-\tfrac34$ in the same units, and the Darwin term contributes $-n$. Their sum is $n-\tfrac34$, again equal to $n/(j+\tfrac12)-\tfrac34$. Thus the combined correction is valid for all allowed $\ell,j$:
$$
\boxed{\Delta E_{\mathrm{fs}}
=E_n^{(0)}\frac{\alpha^2}{n^2}
\left(\frac{n}{j+\tfrac12}-\frac34\right).}
$$
The energy through first order in the fine-structure terms is therefore
$$
E_{nj}\simeq E_n^{(0)}\left[1+\frac{\alpha^2}{n^2}
\left(\frac{n}{j+\tfrac12}-\frac34\right)\right].
$$
At this order the correction depends on $n$ and $j$, with no separate dependence on $\ell$ or $m_j$.
